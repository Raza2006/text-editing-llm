# Using the paper assistant (one page)

**Log in.** Open the address your administrator gave you. The first time, the browser warns that the connection is not private. Click **Advanced → Continue**. Log in with your email and the password you were given, then change it under **your name (bottom left) → Settings → Account**.

**Pick a mode.** At the top of a new chat, choose what you want:
- **Copy-edit** fixes errors only.
- **Clarity and flow** smooths awkward sentences.
- **Make concise** cuts length.
- **Match a reference style** edits your text to sound like a paper you admire.
- **Polish an abstract** restructures and tightens an abstract.
- **Reviewer feedback** gives comments, not rewrites.
- **General writing assistant** answers any writing question.

**Paste your text and press Enter.** You don't need to write instructions; the mode already has them. Your LaTeX, citations, math and numbers are kept exactly. For the best results, send **one section at a time**, even if the whole paper would fit.

**Copy the result** with the copy button under the answer. It copies the raw text, LaTeX included, ready for Overleaf. Don't select the text with the mouse: formulas may copy incorrectly.

**Using a reference paper.** Start your message with `[DOCUMENT]`, then paste the reference paper. The assistant keeps documents marked this way word for word, even when a long chat is summarised. Then send your own sections, one at a time.

**Think** (the switch next to the message box): makes the assistant reason step by step before answering. Use it for hard questions or deep reviews. It's slower, so leave it off for normal editing.

**Compress** (the button under an answer): if a chat gets very long, this summarises its older part before your next message, so the chat stays fast. Very long chats are also compressed automatically. If a chat is ever "too long", start a new chat.

**Your chats are private.** Only you can see them, and they're saved on your lab's computer, not on the internet.

**Always read the edits before using them.** The assistant is good, but it can make mistakes.
